📌 Language Standards (L): Conventions of Standard English 9 standards
L.8.1 Demonstrate command of grammar and usage 4 sub-parts
Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English grammar and usage when writing or speaking.
L.8.1a
Explain function of verbals
Explain the function of verbals (gerunds, participles, infinitives) in general and their function in particular sentences.
L.8.1b
Form and use verbs in active and passive voice
Form and use verbs in the active and passive voice.
L.8.1c
Form and use verbs in indicative, imperative, other moods
Form and use verbs in the indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive mood.
L.8.1d
Recognize and correct shifts in verb voice and mood
Recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb voice and mood.*
L.8.2 Demonstrate command of capitalization, punctuation, spelling 3 sub-parts
Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English capitalization, punctuation, and spelling when writing.
L.8.2a
Use punctuation to indicate a pause or break
Use punctuation (comma, ellipsis, dash) to indicate a pause or break.
L.8.2b
Use an ellipsis to indicate an omission
Use an ellipsis to indicate an omission.
L.8.2c
Spell correctly
Spell correctly.
📌 Language Standards (L): Knowledge of Language 2 standards
L.8.3 Use knowledge of language and its conventions 1 sub-part
Use knowledge of language and its conventions when writing, speaking, reading, or listening.
L.8.3a
Use active/passive voice and mood to achieve effects
Use verbs in the active and passive voice and in the conditional and subjunctive mood to achieve particular effects (e.g., emphasizing the actor or the action; expressing uncertainty or describing a state contrary to fact).
📌 Language Standards (L): Vocabulary Acquisition and Use 10 standards
L.8.4 Determine meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning words 4 sub-parts
Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown and multiple-meaning words or phrases based on grade 8 reading and content, choosing flexibly from a range of strategies.
L.8.4a
Use context as a clue to meaning
Use context (e.g., the overall meaning of a sentence or paragraph; a word's position or function in a sentence) as a clue to the meaning of a word or phrase.
L.8.4b
Use Greek or Latin affixes and roots as clues
Use common, grade-appropriate Greek or Latin affixes and roots as clues to the meaning of a word (e.g., precede, recede, secede).
L.8.4c
Consult reference materials for pronunciation and meaning
Consult general and specialized reference materials (e.g., dictionaries, glossaries, thesauruses), both print and digital, to find the pronunciation of a word or determine or clarify its precise meaning or its part of speech.
L.8.4d
Verify preliminary determination of meaning
Verify the preliminary determination of the meaning of a word or phrase (e.g., by checking the inferred meaning in context or in a dictionary).
L.8.5 Demonstrate understanding of figurative language 3 sub-parts
Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships, and nuances in word meanings.
L.8.5a
Interpret figures of speech in context
Interpret figures of speech (e.g. verbal irony, puns) in context.
L.8.5b
Use word relationships to better understand words
Use the relationship between particular words to better understand each of the words.
L.8.5c
Distinguish connotations of words with similar denotations
Distinguish among the connotations (associations) of words with similar denotations (definitions) (e.g., bullheaded, willful, firm, persistent, resolute).
L.8.6
Acquire and use grade-appropriate domain-specific words
Acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general academic and domain-specific words and phrases; gather vocabulary knowledge when considering a word or phrase important to comprehension or expression.
📌 Reading Standards for Informational Text (RI): Craft and Structure 3 standards
RI.8.4
Analyze impact of word choice including allusions
Determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text, including figurative, connotative, and technical meanings; analyze the impact of specific word choices on meaning and tone, including analogies or allusions to other texts.
RI.8.5
Analyze structure of a specific paragraph in detail
Analyze in detail the structure of a specific paragraph in a text, including the role of particular sentences in developing and refining a key concept.
RI.8.6
Analyze how author responds to conflicting evidence
Determine an author's point of view or purpose in a text and analyze how the author acknowledges and responds to conflicting evidence or viewpoints.
📌 Reading Standards for Informational Text (RI): Integration of Knowledge and Ideas 3 standards
RI.8.7
Evaluate advantages/disadvantages of different mediums
Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of using different mediums (e.g., print or digital text, video, multimedia) to present a particular topic or idea.
RI.8.8
Delineate and evaluate argument, recognizing irrelevant evidence
Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether the reasoning is sound and the evidence is relevant and sufficient; recognize when irrelevant evidence is introduced.
RI.8.9
Analyze conflicting texts on the same topic
Analyze a case in which two or more texts provide conflicting information on the same topic and identify where the texts disagree on matters of fact or interpretation.
📌 Reading Standards for Informational Text (RI): Key Ideas and Details 3 standards
RI.8.1
Cite the strongest textual evidence
Cite the textual evidence that most strongly supports an analysis of what the text says explicitly as well as inferences drawn from the text.
RI.8.2
Determine central idea's relationship to supporting ideas
Determine a central idea of a text and analyze its development over the course of the text, including its relationship to supporting ideas; provide an objective summary of the text.
RI.8.3
Analyze connections and distinctions the text makes
Analyze how a text makes connections among and distinctions between individuals, ideas, or events (e.g., through comparisons, analogies, or categories).
📌 Reading Standards for Informational Text (RI): Range of Reading and Level of Text Complexity 1 standard
RI.8.10
Read and comprehend literary nonfiction at high end of 6-8 band
By the end of the year, read and comprehend literary nonfiction at the high end of the grades 6–8 text complexity band independently and proficiently.
📌 Reading Standards for Literature (RL): Craft and Structure 3 standards
RL.8.4
Analyze impact of word choice including allusions
Determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text, including figurative and connotative meanings; analyze the impact of specific word choices on meaning and tone, including analogies or allusions to other texts.
RL.8.5
Compare and contrast structure of two or more texts
Compare and contrast the structure of two or more texts and analyze how the differing structure of each text contributes to its meaning and style.
RL.8.6
Analyze how differing points of view create suspense/humor
Analyze how differences in the points of view of the characters and the audience or reader (e.g., created through the use of dramatic irony) create such effects as suspense or humor.
📌 Reading Standards for Literature (RL): Integration of Knowledge and Ideas 3 standards
RL.8.7
Analyze fidelity of filmed/live production to the text
Analyze the extent to which a filmed or live production of a story or drama stays faithful to or departs from the text or script, evaluating the choices made by the director or actors.
RL.8.8
Not applicable to literature
(Not applicable to literature)
RL.8.9
Analyze how modern fiction draws on myths and traditions
Analyze how a modern work of fiction draws on themes, patterns of events, or character types from myths, traditional stories, or religious works such as the Bible, including describing how the material is rendered new.
📌 Reading Standards for Literature (RL): Key Ideas and Details 3 standards
RL.8.1
Cite the strongest textual evidence
Cite the textual evidence that most strongly supports an analysis of what the text says explicitly as well as inferences drawn from the text.
RL.8.2
Determine theme's relationship to characters, setting, plot
Determine a theme or central idea of a text and analyze its development over the course of the text, including its relationship to the characters, setting, and plot; provide an objective summary of the text.
RL.8.3
Analyze how dialogue or incidents propel the action
Analyze how particular lines of dialogue or incidents in a story or drama propel the action, reveal aspects of a character, or provoke a decision.
📌 Reading Standards for Literature (RL): Range of Reading and Level of Text Complexity 1 standard
RL.8.10
Read and comprehend literature at high end of 6-8 band
By the end of the year, read and comprehend literature, including stories, dramas, and poems, at the high end of grades 6–8 text complexity band independently and proficiently.
📌 Speaking and Listening Standards (SL): Comprehension and Collaboration 7 standards
SL.8.1 Engage effectively in collaborative discussions 4 sub-parts
Engage effectively in a range of collaborative discussions (one-on-one, in groups, and teacher-led) with diverse partners on grade 8 topics, texts, and issues, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly.
SL.8.1a
Come to discussions prepared, drawing on evidence
Come to discussions prepared, having read or researched material under study; explicitly draw on that preparation by referring to evidence on the topic, text, or issue to probe and reflect on ideas under discussion.
SL.8.1b
Follow rules for discussions and decision-making
Follow rules for collegial discussions and decision-making, track progress toward specific goals and deadlines, and define individual roles as needed.
SL.8.1c
Pose questions connecting the ideas of several speakers
Pose questions that connect the ideas of several speakers and respond to others' questions and comments with relevant evidence, observations, and ideas.
SL.8.1d
Qualify or justify views in light of evidence presented
Acknowledge new information expressed by others, and, when warranted, qualify or justify their own views in light of the evidence presented.
SL.8.2
Analyze purpose of information and evaluate motives
Analyze the purpose of information presented in diverse media and formats (e.g., visually, quantitatively, orally) and evaluate the motives (e.g., social, commercial, political) behind its presentation.
SL.8.3
Delineate speaker's argument, identifying irrelevant evidence
Delineate a speaker's argument and specific claims, evaluating the soundness of the reasoning and relevance and sufficiency of the evidence and identifying when irrelevant evidence is introduced.
📌 Speaking and Listening Standards (SL): Presentation of Knowledge and Ideas 3 standards
SL.8.4
Present claims with relevant evidence and sound reasoning
Present claims and findings, emphasizing salient points in a focused, coherent manner with relevant evidence, sound valid reasoning, and well-chosen details; use appropriate eye contact, adequate volume, and clear pronunciation.
SL.8.5
Integrate multimedia to strengthen claims and add interest
Integrate multimedia and visual displays into presentations to clarify information, strengthen claims and evidence, and add interest.
SL.8.6
Adapt speech using formal English when appropriate
Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when indicated or appropriate. (See grade 8 Language standards 1 and 3 on page 52 for specific expectations.)
📌 Writing Standards (W): Production and Distribution of Writing 3 standards
W.8.4
Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task
Produce clear and coherent writing in which the development, organization, and style are appropriate to task, purpose, and audience. (Grade-specific expectations for writing types are defined in standards 1–3 above.)
W.8.5
Strengthen writing focusing on purpose and audience
With some guidance and support from peers and adults, develop and strengthen writing as needed by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach, focusing on how well purpose and audience have been addressed. (Editing for conventions should demonstrate command of Language standards 1–3 up to and including grade 8 on page 52.)
W.8.6
Use technology to present relationships efficiently
Use technology, including the Internet, to produce and publish writing and present the relationships between information and ideas efficiently as well as to interact and collaborate with others.
📌 Writing Standards (W): Range of Writing 1 standard
W.8.10
Write routinely over varied time frames
Write routinely over extended time frames (time for research, reflection, and revision) and shorter time frames (a single sitting or a day or two) for a range of discipline-specific tasks, purposes, and audiences.
📌 Writing Standards (W): Research to Build and Present Knowledge 5 standards
W.8.7
Conduct short research projects with self-generated questions
Conduct short research projects to answer a question (including a self-generated question), drawing on several sources and generating additional related, focused questions that allow for multiple avenues of exploration.
W.8.8
Gather information using effective search terms and citation
Gather relevant information from multiple print and digital sources, using search terms effectively; assess the credibility and accuracy of each source; and quote or paraphrase the data and conclusions of others while avoiding plagiarism and following a standard format for citation.
W.8.9 Draw evidence from texts to support analysis 2 sub-parts
Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection, and research.
W.8.9a
Apply grade 8 Reading standards to literature
Apply grade 8 Reading standards to literature (e.g., "Analyze how a modern work of fiction draws on themes, patterns of events, or character types from myths, traditional stories, or religious works such as the Bible, including describing how the material is rendered new").
W.8.9b
Apply grade 8 Reading standards to literary nonfiction
Apply grade 8 Reading standards to literary nonfiction (e.g., "Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether the reasoning is sound and the evidence is relevant and sufficient; recognize when irrelevant evidence is introduced").
📌 Writing Standards (W): Text Types and Purposes 19 standards
W.8.1 Write arguments with clear reasons and relevant evidence 5 sub-parts
Write arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence.
W.8.1a
Introduce claims, distinguish from opposing claims logically
Introduce claim(s), acknowledge and distinguish the claim(s) from alternate or opposing claims, and organize the reasons and evidence logically.
W.8.1b
Support claims with logical reasoning and credible sources
Support claim(s) with logical reasoning and relevant evidence, using accurate, credible sources and demonstrating an understanding of the topic or text.
W.8.1c
Use words/phrases/clauses to clarify counterclaims
Use words, phrases, and clauses to create cohesion and clarify the relationships among claim(s), counterclaims, reasons, and evidence.
W.8.1d
Establish and maintain a formal style
Establish and maintain a formal style.
W.8.1e
Provide concluding statement that supports argument
Provide a concluding statement or section that follows from and supports the argument presented.
W.8.2 Write informative/explanatory texts 6 sub-parts
Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas, concepts, and information through the selection, organization, and analysis of relevant content.
W.8.2a
Introduce topic and organize information into categories
Introduce a topic clearly, previewing what is to follow; organize ideas, concepts, and information into broader categories; include formatting (e.g., headings), graphics (e.g., charts, tables), and multimedia when useful to aiding comprehension.
W.8.2b
Develop topic with well-chosen facts and quotations
Develop the topic with relevant, well-chosen facts, definitions, concrete details, quotations, or other information and examples.
W.8.2c
Use varied transitions to create cohesion
Use appropriate and varied transitions to create cohesion and clarify the relationships among ideas and concepts.
W.8.2d
Use precise language and domain-specific vocabulary
Use precise language and domain-specific vocabulary to inform about or explain the topic.
W.8.2e
Establish and maintain a formal style
Establish and maintain a formal style.
W.8.2f
Provide concluding statement that supports information
Provide a concluding statement or section that follows from and supports the information or explanation presented.
W.8.3 Write narratives with well-structured event sequences 5 sub-parts
Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences or events using effective technique, relevant descriptive details, and well-structured event sequences.
W.8.3a
Engage and orient reader, introduce narrator/characters
Engage and orient the reader by establishing a context and point of view and introducing a narrator and/or characters; organize an event sequence that unfolds naturally and logically.
W.8.3b
Use narrative techniques including reflection
Use narrative techniques, such as dialogue, pacing, description, and reflection, to develop experiences, events, and/or characters.
W.8.3c
Use transition words showing relationships among events
Use a variety of transition words, phrases, and clauses to convey sequence, signal shifts from one time frame or setting to another, and show the relationships among experiences and events.
W.8.3d
Use precise words and sensory language to capture action
Use precise words and phrases, relevant descriptive details, and sensory language to capture the action and convey experiences and events.
W.8.3e
Provide a conclusion that reflects on the narrative
Provide a conclusion that follows from and reflects on the narrated experiences or events.
